## Encryption Scheme

> [!algorithm] Congruential Public-Key Cryptosystem
> **Participants:** Alice (key owner), Bob (sender)
>
> **Output:**  
> Correct encryption and decryption of a plaintext integer $m$
>
> ---
>
> ### Key Creation (Alice)
>
> 1. Choose a large integer modulus $q$.
>
> 2. Choose secret integers $f$ and $g$ such that:
>    $$f < \sqrt{q/2}, \qquad \sqrt{q/4} < g < \sqrt{q/2}, \qquad \gcd(f,g) = 1.$$
>
> 3. Compute:
>    $$h \gets f^{-1} g \pmod q.$$
>
> 4. Publish the public key $(q, h)$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Encryption (Bob)
>
> **Input:** Plaintext $m$ with $m < \sqrt{q/2}$  
> **Public key:** $(q, h)$
>
> 1. Bob computes the ciphertext:
>    $$e \gets r h + m \pmod q,$$
>    where $r$ is a random integer.
>
> 2. Bob sends the ciphertext $e$ to Alice.
>
> ---
>
> ### Decryption (Alice)
>
> 1. Alice computes:
>    $$a \gets f e \pmod q,$$
>    with $0 < a < q$.
>
> 2. Alice computes:
>    $$b \gets f^{-1} a \pmod g,$$
>    with $0 < b < g$.
>
> 3. Output the plaintext:
>    $$m \gets b.$$

## Cryptanalysis

> [!remark]
> Eve knows the public values of $q$ and $h$ and, she wants to recover the private key $f$ that satisfies $h \equiv f^{-1} g \pmod q$. Thus, we want to find small vectors in the lattice $L$ generated by $$v_1 = (1, h) \quad \text{and} \quad v_2 = (0, q),$$ since as we saw, the vector $(f, g)$ is in $L$, and given the size constraints on $f$ and $g$, it is likely to be the shortest nonzero vector in $L$.
