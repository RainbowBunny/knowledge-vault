## Secret Sharing Scheme

> [!algorithm] Secret Sharing Scheme
> A **secret sharing scheme over** $Z$ is a pair of efficient algorithm $(G, C)$:
> - $G$ is a probabilistic **sharing algorithm** that is invoked as $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N) \xleftarrow{R} G(N, t, \alpha)$, where $0 < t \leq N$ and $\alpha \in Z$, to generate a $t$-out-of-$N$ sharing of $\alpha$.
> - $C$ is a deterministic **combining algorithm** that is invoked as $\alpha \leftarrow C(J, \{\alpha_j\}_{j \in J})$, where $J$ is a subset of $\{1, \dots, N\}$ of size $t$, to recover $\alpha$.
> **Correctness**: We require that for every $\alpha \in Z$, for every possible output $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N)$ of $G(N, t, \alpha)$, and every $t$-size subset $J$ of $\{1, \dots, N\}$, we have $$C(J, \{\alpha_j\}_{j \in J}) = \alpha.$$

> [!definition] Secure Secret Sharing Scheme
> A secret sharing scheme $(G, C)$ over $Z$ is **secure** if, for
> - all allowable parameters $0 < t \leq N$,
> - every $\alpha, \alpha' \in Z$, and
> - every subset $J$ of $\{1, \dots, N\}$ of size $t - 1$,
>
> the distribution $G(N, t, \alpha)[J]$ is identical to the distribution $G(N, t, \alpha')[J]$; here $G(N, t, \alpha)[J]$ is the tuple of shares $(\alpha_j)_{j \in J}$. `[BS §22.1]`
>
> A statement about distributions with no adversary (perfect security), so it is not drawn as a game.

### Shamir Secret Sharing

> [!algorithm] Shamir Secret Sharing
> ### Algorithm
> - $G_{sh}(N, t, \alpha)$: 
> 	1. Chooses random $k_1, \dots, k_{t - 1} \xleftarrow{R} \mathbb Z_q$ and define the polynomial $$\omega(x) = k_{t - 1} x^{t - 1} + k_{t - 2} x^{t - 2} + \dots + k_1 x + \alpha \in \mathbb Z_q[x].$$ 
> 	2. Notice that $\omega$ has degree at most $t - 1$ and that $\omega(0) = \alpha$. For $i = 1, \dots, N$ compute $\alpha_i \leftarrow \omega(i) \in \mathbb Z_q$. 
> 	3. Output the $N$ shares $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N$.
> - $C_{sh}(J, \{\alpha_j\}_{j \in J})$: Since every polynomial of degree at most $t - 1$ is determined by its value at $t$ points, the $t$ shares $\alpha_j$ for $j \in J$ completely determine the polynomial $\omega$. Algorithm $C_{sh}$ interpolates the polynomial $\omega$ and outputs the constant term $\alpha = \omega(0)$.

> [!remark] Notation in the literature
> `[Sha79]` calls this a $(k, n)$ threshold scheme: the secret $D$ is split into $n$ pieces $D_i = q(i)$ of a random polynomial $q$ of degree $k - 1$ with $q(0) = D$, modulo a prime $p$ larger than $D$ and $n$. Here $k = t$, $n = N$, $D = \alpha$, $p = q$ and the polynomial is $\omega$. Its security condition, that any $k - 1$ pieces leave all values of $D$ equally likely, is the theorem below.

> [!theorem] Security of Shamir Secret Sharing
> Shamir's secret sharing scheme $(G_{sh}, C_{sh})$ is secure. In particular, if $0 < t \leq N < q$, then for every $\alpha \in \mathbb Z_q$, any collection of $t - 1$ shares $\{\alpha_\ell\}_{\ell \in \mathcal L}$ is a mutually independent family of random variables, with each share uniformly distributed over $\mathbb Z_q$.

## Threshold Signature

> [!algorithm] Threshold Signature
> A **threshold signature scheme** $(\mathsf{KeyGen}, S, V, C)$ is a tuple of four efficient algorithms:
> - $\mathsf{KeyGen}$ is a probabilistic **key generation algorithm** that is invoked as $$(pk, pkc, sk_1, \dots, sk_N) \xleftarrow{R} \mathsf{KeyGen}(N, t)$$ to generate a $t$-out-of-$N$ shared key. It outputs a **public key** $pk$, a **combiner public key** $pkc$, and $N$ **signing key shares**, $sk_1, \dots, sk_N$.
> - $S$ is a (possibly) probabilistic **signing algorithm** that is invoked as $\sigma_i' \xleftarrow{R} S(sk_i, m)$, where $sk_i$ is one of the key shares generated by $\mathsf{KeyGen}$, $m$ is a message, and $\sigma_i'$ is a signature share for $m$ using $sk_i$.
> - $V$ is a deterministic **verification algorithm** as in a signature scheme, invoked as $V(pk, m, \sigma)$ and outputs either $\text{accept}$ or $\text{reject}$.
> - $C$ is a deterministic **combiner algorithm** that is invoked as $\sigma \leftarrow C(pkc, m, J, \{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J})$, where $pkc$ is the combiner public key, $m$ is a message, $J$ is a subset of $\{1, \dots, N\}$ of size $t$, and each $\sigma_j'$ is a signature share for $m$. The algorithm either outputs a signature $\sigma$, or outputs a special message $\text{blame}(J^*)$, where $J^*$ is a nonempty subset of $J$. 
> Intuitively, the message $\text{blame}(J^*)$ indicates that the provided signature shares $\sigma_j'$ for $j \in J^*$, are invalid.
> **Correctness**: as usual, the verification algorithm should accept a properly constructed signature; specifically, for all possible outputs $(pk, pkc, sk_1, \dots, sk_N)$ of $\mathsf{KeyGen}(N, t)$, all messages $m$, and all $t$-size subsets $J$ of $\{1, \dots, N\}$, we have $$P[V(pk, m, C(pkc, m, J, \{S(sk_j, m)\}_{j \in J})) = \text{accept}] = 1$$

> [!remark] Notation in the literature
> `[BS §22.2]` writes the scheme as $(G, S, V, C)$ with key generation $G$. The vault calls key generation $\mathsf{KeyGen}$ ([[Note Layouts]] § *Game form*). The sharing algorithm $G$ of a secret sharing scheme keeps its name: it splits a secret and generates no key.

### Generic Threshold Signature Scheme

> [!algorithm] Generic Threshold Signature Scheme
> Every secure signature scheme $(\mathsf{KeyGen}, S, V)$ can be trivially transformed into a threshold signature scheme $(\mathsf{KeyGen}', S', V', C')$ as follows:
> - During setup, the key generator creates $N$ key pairs $(pk_i, sk_i) \xleftarrow{R} \mathsf{KeyGen}()$, for $i = 1, \dots, N$, and gives key $sk_i$ to signing server $i$. The public verification key is $pk = (pk_1, \dots, pk_N)$, and the combiner public key is the same, $pkc = pk$. More precisely, we define threshold key generation $\mathsf{KeyGen}'(N, t)$ as an algorithm that outputs $(pk, pk, sk_1, \dots, sk_N)$.
> - To sign a message $m$, signing server $i$, for $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$, outputs the signature share $\sigma_i' \xleftarrow{R} S(sk_i, m)$.
> - The combiner collects $t$ signature shares from the signing servers, and outputs all $t$ of them as the full signature. In more detail, algorithm $C'(pkc, m, J, \{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J})$, where $pkc = (pk_1, \dots, pk_N)$ and $|J| = t$, first checks that each $\sigma_j'$ is a valid signature on $m$ under $pk_j$. If not, it outputs $\text{blame}(J^*)$, where $J^*$ is the set of indices $j$ for which $\sigma_j'$ is invalid. Otherwise, it outputs the signature $\sigma = (J, \{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J})$.
> - Finally, algorithm $V'(pk, m, \sigma)$, where $pk = (pk_1, \dots, pk_N)$ and $\sigma = (J, \{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J})$, outputs $\text{accept}$ if $|J| = t$ and each $\sigma_j'$ is a valid signature on $m$ under $pk_j$.

### Threshold Signature Security

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $\mathcal S = (\mathsf{KeyGen}, S, V, C)$: a threshold signature scheme defined over $(\mathcal M, \Sigma)$.
> - $N, t$: the number of signing servers and the threshold, poly-bounded with $0 < t \leq N$, chosen by the adversary.
> - $\mathcal L \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ with $|\mathcal L| = t - 1$: the corrupted servers, whose key shares the adversary gets.
> - $m_1, m_2, \dots$: the signing queries. $(m, \sigma)$: the candidate forgery.
> - $\mathbb A$: the class of efficient [[Adversary|adversaries]].

> [!definition] Threshold Signature Security
> ### Scope
> A threshold signature scheme $\mathcal S$ and the adversary class $\mathbb A$ of Parameters.
>
> ---
> ### Condition
> - **Access** — $pk$, $pkc$ and the key shares $sk_\ell$ for $\ell \in \mathcal L$; a signing oracle that returns all $N$ signature shares on any message.
> - **Promise**:
>     - $0 < t \leq N$, both poly-bounded;
>     - $|\mathcal L| = t - 1$.
> - **Quantifier** — $\forall \mathcal A \in \mathbb A$.
> - **Adaptivity** — static corruption: $\mathcal A$ fixes $N$, $t$ and $\mathcal L$ before the keys exist. The signing queries are adaptive.
>
> ---
> ### Experiment
> A search game: one branch; the challenger checks the forgery in the last row. `[BS §22.2]`
>
> | | challenger $\mathcal C$ | | adversary $\mathcal A$ |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | 1 | | $\xleftarrow{\ N,\, t,\, \mathcal L\ }$ | chooses allowable $N, t$ and a set $\mathcal L \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ of size $t - 1$ |
> | 2 | $(pk, pkc, sk_1, \dots, sk_N) \xleftarrow{\$} \mathsf{KeyGen}(N, t)$ | $\xrightarrow{\ pk,\, pkc,\, \{sk_\ell\}_{\ell \in \mathcal L}\ }$ | |
> | 3 | | $\xleftarrow{\ m_j\ }$ | **Signing queries** (repeated, adaptive): sends $m_j \in \mathcal M$ |
> | | $\sigma'_{j, i} \xleftarrow{\$} S(sk_i, m_j)$ for $i = 1, \dots, N$ | $\xrightarrow{\ \sigma'_{j, 1}, \dots, \sigma'_{j, N}\ }$ | |
> | 4 | | $\xleftarrow{\ m,\, \sigma\ }$ | outputs a candidate forgery $(m, \sigma) \in \mathcal M \times \Sigma$ |
> | 5 | checks whether $V(pk, m, \sigma) = \text{accept}$<br>checks whether $m \notin \{m_1, m_2, \dots\}$ | | |
>
> **Goal** — $\mathcal A$ wins iff both hold:
> - $V(pk, m, \sigma) = \text{accept}$;
> - $m$ is new: $m \notin \{m_1, m_2, \dots\}$.
>
> ---
> ### Property
> $$\text{thSIGadv}[\mathcal A, \mathcal S] = \Pr[\mathcal A \text{ wins}].$$
> - $\mathcal S$ is **secure** if $\text{thSIGadv}[\mathcal A, \mathcal S]$ is negligible for every efficient $\mathcal A$.
>
> It is a *search* game, see [[Security Game]].

> [!remark] Notation in the literature
> - `[BS §22.2]` splits the game into phases — *Setup*, *Signing queries*, *Forgery* — which are rows 1–2, row 3 and row 4 here.
> - `[Bol03 Def 3.1]` counts differently: the adversary corrupts up to $t$ of $n$ players, chosen in advance, and $t + 1$ shares are needed to sign. It sees the view of the key generation protocol, a distributed protocol instead of a trusted $\mathsf{KeyGen}$, and of the signing protocol run on messages of its choice; it wins with a valid signature on a message it never submitted for signing.

### Robust Threshold Signatures

> [!definition] Robust Scheme
> Suppose a combiner receives a collection $\{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J}$ of $t$ signature shares on a given message $m$. The combiner will output either a signature $\sigma$, or a special message $\text{blame}(J^*)$, where $J^*$ is a nonempty subset of $J$.
> - If the output of the combiner is $\text{blame}(J^*)$, then we would like it to be the case that all of the signature shares $\sigma_j'$ for $j \in J^*$ are "bad", in the sense that they were incorrectly generated by misbehaving signing servers. A threshold signature scheme that guarantees that this always happens is said to provide **accurate blaming**.
> - Otherwise, the output of the combiner is a signature $\sigma$ on the given message $m$. A threshold signature scheme is said to be **consistent** if no efficient adversary can cause the combiner to output a signature that is invalid on $m$, with non-negligible probability. When the combiner outputs a valid signature we say that the given collection of shares $\{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J}$ is **valid** for $m$.
> 
> A threshold signature scheme is **robust** if it satisfies both properties.

> [!definition] Accurate Blaming
> We say that a threshold signature scheme $\mathcal S = (\mathsf{KeyGen}, S, V, C)$ provides **accurate blaming** if, for
> - all possible outputs $(pk, pkc, sk_1, \dots, sk_N)$ of $\mathsf{KeyGen}(N, t)$,
> - all messages $m$,
> - all $t$-size subsets $J$ of $\{1, \dots, N\}$, and
> - all collections of signature shares $\{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J}$,
>
> whenever
> $$C(pkc, m, J, \{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J}) = \text{blame}(J^*),$$
> every blamed share is one that $S$ never outputs:
> $$\Pr[S(sk_j, m) = \sigma_j'] = 0 \quad \text{for every } j \in J^*.$$
>
> A perfect property with no adversary, so it is not drawn as a game.

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $\mathcal S = (\mathsf{KeyGen}, S, V, C)$, $N$, $t$: as for threshold signature security.
> - $m$: a message. $J \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ with $|J| = t$. $\{\sigma'_j\}_{j \in J}$: signature shares. All three are chosen by the adversary.
> - $\mathbb A$: the class of efficient [[Adversary|adversaries]].

> [!definition] Consistent Threshold Signatures
> ### Scope
> A threshold signature scheme $\mathcal S$ and the adversary class $\mathbb A$ of Parameters.
>
> ---
> ### Condition
> - **Access** — every key: $pk$, $pkc$ and all $N$ key shares. No oracle; with the keys, $\mathcal A$ can compute any signature share itself.
> - **Promise**:
>     - $0 < t \leq N$, both poly-bounded;
>     - $|J| = t$.
> - **Quantifier** — $\forall \mathcal A \in \mathbb A$.
> - **Adaptivity** — $\mathcal A$ chooses $m$, $J$ and the shares after it has seen all the keys.
>
> ---
> ### Experiment
> A search game: one branch; the challenger runs the combiner and checks its output in the last row. `[BS §22.2]`
>
> | | challenger $\mathcal C$ | | adversary $\mathcal A$ |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | 1 | | $\xleftarrow{\ N,\, t\ }$ | chooses allowable $N, t$ |
> | 2 | $(pk, pkc, sk_1, \dots, sk_N) \xleftarrow{\$} \mathsf{KeyGen}(N, t)$ | $\xrightarrow{\ pk,\, pkc,\, sk_1, \dots, sk_N\ }$ | |
> | 3 | | $\xleftarrow{\ m,\, J,\, \{\sigma'_j\}_{j \in J}\ }$ | outputs a message $m$, a set $J \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ of size $t$ and signature shares $\{\sigma'_j\}_{j \in J}$ |
> | 4 | $\mathrm{out} \leftarrow C(pkc, m, J, \{\sigma'_j\}_{j \in J})$ | | |
>
> **Goal** — $\mathcal A$ wins iff both hold:
> - $\mathrm{out}$ is a signature $\sigma$, not a message $\text{blame}(J^*)$;
> - $V(pk, m, \sigma) = \text{reject}$.
>
> ---
> ### Property
> $$\text{conSIGadv}[\mathcal A, \mathcal S] = \Pr[\mathcal A \text{ wins}].$$
> - $\mathcal S$ is **consistent** if $\text{conSIGadv}[\mathcal A, \mathcal S]$ is negligible for every efficient $\mathcal A$.
>
> It is a *search* game, see [[Security Game]].

> [!definition] Robustness
> We say that a threshold signature scheme is **robust** if it provides accurate blaming and is consistent.

### BLS Threshold Signing

> [!algorithm] BLS Threshold Signature Scheme
> ### Building Block
> - Group $\mathbb G$.
> - Hash function $H: \mathcal M \rightarrow \mathbb G$.
> - BLS signature scheme
> 
> ---
> ### Algorithm
> We describe the BLS threshold signature scheme $\mathcal S_{thBLS} = (\mathsf{KeyGen}', S', V', C')$:
> - $(pk, pkc, sk_1, \dots, sk_N) \leftarrow \mathsf{KeyGen}'(N, t)$:
> 	1. Choose a random $\alpha \xleftarrow{R} \mathbb Z_q$ and compute $pk \leftarrow g^{\alpha}$;
> 	2. Run $G_{sh}(N, t, \alpha)$ to obtain $N$ shares $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N \in \mathbb Z_q$;
> 	3. For $i = 1, \dots, N$, compute $u_i \leftarrow g^{\alpha_i}$, and set $sk_i = \alpha_i$ and $pk_i = u_i$; 
> 	4. Set $pkc = (pk_1, \dots, pk_N)$;
> 	5. Output $(pk, pkc, sk_1, \dots, sk_N)$.
> - $\sigma_i' \leftarrow S'(sk_i, m)$, where $sk_i = \alpha_i$: output the signature share $\sigma_i' \leftarrow H(m)^{\alpha_i} \in \mathbb G$.
> - $\sigma \leftarrow C'(pkc, m, J, \{\sigma_j'\}_{j \in J})$, where $J$ is a subset of $\{1, \dots, N\}$ of size $t$, and $$pkc = (pk_1, \dots, pk_N) = (u_1, \dots, u_N):$$
> 	- Step 1: Verify that all $t$ signature shares are valid: let $J^*$ be the set of all $j \in J$ such that $$V_{BLS}(u_j, m, \sigma_j') = \text{reject};$$ if $J^*$ is nonempty, output $\text{blame}(J^*)$ and abort;
> 	- Step 2: Compute the signature: Interpolate in the exponent with the given set $J$ and $j^* = 0$ to compute the signature $\sigma = H(m)^{\alpha} = H(m)^{\omega(0)}$ from the $t$ group elements $\sigma_j' = H(m)^{\omega(j)}$ for $j \in J$; output $\sigma$ as the signature on $m$; more explicitly, the signature $\sigma$ is computed as $$\sigma \leftarrow \prod_{j \in J}(\sigma_j')^{\lambda_j} \in \mathbb G$$ where the exponents $\{\lambda_j\}_{j \in J}$ are the Lagrange interpolation coefficients.
> - $V'(pk, m, \sigma)$: Output $V_{BLS}(pk, m, \sigma)$.

> [!theorem]
> If $\mathcal S_{BLS}$ is a secure signature scheme, then $\mathcal S_{thBLS}$ is a secure threshold signature scheme.

> [!remark] The same scheme in `[Bol03]`
> `[Bol03 §3.2]` builds threshold BLS with the distributed key generation of Gennaro, Jarecki, Krawczyk and Rabin instead of a trusted $\mathsf{KeyGen}'$, and proves it unforgeable and robust in its own sense of Definition 3.1. `⚠️ CLAUDE – verify:` the proof is in the random-oracle model, assuming the computational Diffie–Hellman problem is hard in a gap Diffie–Hellman group.
