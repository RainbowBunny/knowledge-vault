## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $(R, +, \cdot)$: a [[Ring]].
> - $I \subseteq R$: a [[Subgroup]] of $(R, +)$.

> [!definition] Ideal
> Extends:: [[Subgroup]].
> Requires:: [[Ring]].
>
> ---
> A [[Subgroup]] $I$ of $(R, +)$ is
> - a **left ideal** of $R$ if $rI \subseteq I$ for all $r \in R$:
> $$(\forall r \in R)(\forall a \in I): ra \in I$$
> - a **right ideal** of $R$ if $Ir \subseteq I$ for all $r \in R$:
> $$(\forall r \in R)(\forall a \in I): ar \in I$$
> - a **two-sided ideal** if both.
>
> `[Alu III.3.1]` `[Jud Rem 16.28]`

### Principal Ideal

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $a \in R$: an element.

> [!definition] Ideal generated by an element
> Let $a \in R$ be any element of [[Ring]] $(R, +, \cdot)$. Then:
> - $Ra = \{ra \mid r \in R\}$ is a left ideal of $R$ (the left ideal generated by $a$).
> - $aR = \{ar \mid r \in R\}$ is a right ideal of $R$ (the right ideal generated by $a$).
>
> `[Alu III.4.1]`

> [!definition] Principal Ideal
> If $R$ is a [[Commutative Ring]], then $aR = Ra$ and this subset is the **principal ideal generated by** $a$, written $(a)$ (Judson: $\langle a \rangle$). In particular $(1) = R$.
>
> `[Alu III.4.1]` `[Jud §16.3]`

## Variant

> [!remark] Ideals as subrings
> In `[Jud §16.3]` a ring need not have an identity, and an ideal is defined as a *subring* $I$ with $ar, ra \in I$ for $a \in I$, $r \in R$. Here rings have $1$ and a [[Subring]] must contain $1$, so the only ideal that is a subring is $R$ itself; that is why the definition above asks only for a subgroup of $(R, +)$.

## Example

> [!example] Ideals of $\mathbb Z$
> Every ideal of $\mathbb Z$ is principal: it is $n\mathbb Z = (n)$ for some $n \geq 0$. `[Jud Thm 16.25]`
