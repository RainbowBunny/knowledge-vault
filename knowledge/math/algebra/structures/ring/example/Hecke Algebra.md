## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $(W, I)$: a Coxeter group — a group $W$ generated by a set $I = \{s_1, \dots, s_r\}$ of elements of order $2$; $m(i, j)$: the order of $s_i s_j$ for $i \neq j$. `[Bum, Introduction]`
> - $q$: the parameter. For $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over $\mathbb F_q$ it is the size of the field, and $W = S_n$ ([[Symmetric Group]]).

> [!definition] Iwahori–Hecke Algebra
> $\mathcal H_q(W)$ is the algebra with generators $T_1, \dots, T_r$ and relations
> - **braid**: $T_i T_j T_i \cdots = T_j T_i T_j \cdots$, both sides with $m(i, j)$ factors;
> - **quadratic**: $T_i^2 = (q - 1) T_i + q$, i.e. $(T_i - q)(T_i + 1) = 0$, in place of $s_i^2 = 1$.
>
> `[Bum, Introduction]`

## Property

### A deformation of the group algebra

> [!proposition] At $q = 1$ it is the group algebra — $\mathcal H_1(W) \cong \mathbb C[W]$
> For $q = 1$ the quadratic relation becomes $T_i^2 = 1$, so $\mathcal H_1(W)$ is isomorphic to the [[Group Algebra]] $\mathbb C[W]$; in general $\mathcal H_q(W)$ is a deformation of $\mathbb C[W]$ `[Bum, Introduction]`. It was introduced by Iwahori, and its definition rests on the [[Matrix Group#Bruhat Decomposition|Bruhat decomposition]] `[Lus §3]`.

### Finite groups of Lie type

> [!definition] Hecke Algebra of a Subgroup
> For a finite group $G$ and a subgroup $K$, the Hecke algebra $\mathcal H_K$ is the space of $K$-bi-invariant functions on $G$ — $\varphi(k g k') = \varphi(g)$ — under convolution `[Bum §2]`. It is isomorphic to the ring of $G$-endomorphisms of the induced representation $\mathrm{Ind}_K^G \mathbf 1$ `[Bum Prop 4; CasH Lemma 1.1]`.

> [!theorem] Iwahori's theorem — $\mathcal H(G /\!/ B)$ is the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of the Weyl group
> ### Scope
> $G$ the group of $\mathbb F_q$-points of a connected reductive group, $B$ a Borel subgroup, $W$ the Weyl group — e.g. $G = \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$, $B$ the upper triangular matrices, $W = S_n$.
>
> ---
> ### Property
> The characteristic functions $\tau_w$ of the double cosets $BwB$, $w \in W$, form a basis of $\mathcal H(G /\!/ B)$ — by the Bruhat decomposition, $G$ is the disjoint union of the [[Coset#Double Coset|double cosets]] $BwB$. They satisfy $\tau_s^2 = (q - 1)\tau_s + q\,\tau_1$ for the simple reflections $s$, and product rules for $\tau_w \tau_s$ that determine the multiplication completely. `[CasH §1, Lemma 1.3, Prop 1.4]`

### $p$-adic groups: the affine Hecke algebra

> [!definition] Iwahori Subgroup
> Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field with ring of integers $\mathfrak o$ and residue field $\mathfrak o / \mathfrak p = \mathbb F_q$ — e.g. $F = \mathbb Q_p$, $\mathfrak o = \mathbb Z_p$, $q = p$ ([[p-adic Number]]). $K^\circ = \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathfrak o)$ is a maximal compact subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(n, F)$. The **Iwahori subgroup** $J$ is the set of $k \in K^\circ$ whose image $\bar k$ under $\mathrm{GL}(n, \mathfrak o) \to \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb F_q)$ is upper triangular. `[Bum §1]`

> [!remark] The reduction map is onto
> $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathfrak o)$ maps onto $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathfrak o / \mathfrak p^m)$ for every $m$ `[CasG §11]`; for $m = 1$ this is the reduction $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z_p) \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_p)$ through $\mathbb Z_p / p\mathbb Z_p \cong \mathbb F_p$. So the Iwahori subgroup is the full preimage of the Borel subgroup $B(\mathbb F_q)$ `[HKP, Notation]`, and $J \backslash K^\circ / J$ is in bijection with $B \backslash \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q) / B$.

> [!theorem] Iwahori–Matsumoto — the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of $G(F)$ is an affine Hecke algebra
> The Iwahori–Hecke algebra $\mathcal H = C_c(I \backslash G / I)$ of compactly supported $I$-bi-invariant functions has the basis $T_x = \mathbf 1_{IxI}$, $x \in \tilde W$, where $\tilde W$ is the semidirect product of $W$ and the cocharacter lattice $X_*$ `[HKP §1.1–1.2]`. Iwahori and Matsumoto showed that $\mathcal H_q(W_\mathrm{aff})$ is realised this way `[Bum, Introduction]`. For $\mathrm{GL}_n$: generators $T_0, \dots, T_{n-1}$ and $t$; $T_i$ and $T_j$ commute unless $i \equiv j \pm 1 \bmod n$; $T_i T_{i+1} T_i = T_{i+1} T_i T_{i+1}$ with indices mod $n$; $T_i^2 = (q - 1)T_i + q$; and $t T_i t^{-1} = T_{i+1}$. The Coxeter group of $T_0, \dots, T_{n-1}$ is the affine Weyl group of type $A^{(1)}_{n-1}$. `[Bum §1]`

> [!proposition] The finite Hecke algebra sits inside — $\mathcal H_0 \subseteq \mathcal H$
> $\mathcal H_0 = C(I \backslash K / I)$ is a subalgebra of $\mathcal H$ `[HKP §1.7]`. By the bijection of double cosets above, it is the Hecke algebra $\mathcal H(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q) /\!/ B)$ of Iwahori's theorem.

> [!theorem] Bernstein presentation — a commutative subalgebra and the center
> Let $R = \mathbb C[X_*]$, embedded in $\mathcal H$ by $\mu \mapsto \Theta_\mu$, so that $\Theta_\lambda \Theta_\mu = \Theta_{\lambda + \mu}$.
> - Multiplication gives a vector-space isomorphism $R \otimes \mathcal H_0 \to \mathcal H$, $\pi^\mu \otimes h \mapsto \Theta_\mu h$ `[HKP Lemma 1.7.1]`: the $\Theta_\mu T_w$ form a basis.
> - The Bernstein relation: $T_s \Theta_\mu = \Theta_{s\mu} T_s + (q - 1)\dfrac{\Theta_\mu - \Theta_{s\mu}}{1 - \Theta_{-\check\alpha}}$ for the simple reflection $s = s_\alpha$ `[Pra §3.4; HKP (1.15.2)]`.
> - The center of $\mathcal H$ is $R^W$ `[HKP Lemma 2.3.1]`.
>
> Attributed to Bernstein, in Lusztig, Astérisque 101 (1983) `[Pra §3]`.

> [!theorem] Borel–Casselman equivalence — Iwahori-fixed vectors see the whole block
> The functor $(\pi, V) \mapsto V^I$ is an equivalence between the category of smooth representations of $G$ generated by their $I$-fixed vectors and the category of $\mathcal H$-modules `[Bro §4]`. Borel (1976) set up the correspondence between irreducible admissible representations with nonzero $I$-fixed vectors and irreducible finite-dimensional $\mathcal H$-modules; Casselman showed that these representations are exactly the subquotients of the unramified principal series `[Pra §1.1; Cas80 Prop 2.6]`.

> [!definition] Smooth Representation, Unramified Character, Parabolic Induction
> - A representation $V$ of $G$ is **smooth** if the stabiliser of every vector of $V$ is open `[Her Def 1.2]`.
> - A character of a group $M$ (e.g. the diagonal torus) is **unramified** if it is trivial on its maximal compact subgroup $M^\circ$; the principal series induced from such a character is called unramified `[Cas80 §2]`.
> - **Parabolic induction** induces a representation $\sigma$ of a parabolic subgroup $P$ (e.g. $B$) to $G$: smooth functions $f$ on $G$ with $f(pg) = \sigma(p) f(g)$, with $G$ acting by right translation `[Her §1.4]`.
