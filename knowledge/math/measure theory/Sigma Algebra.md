## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $X$: a set. $\mathcal S$: a set of subsets of $X$, i.e. $\mathcal S \subseteq \mathcal P(X)$, the [[Power Set]].

> [!definition] Sigma Algebra
> $\mathcal S$ is a **σ-algebra** on $X$ if
> - $\emptyset \in \mathcal S$;
> - if $E \in \mathcal S$, then $X \setminus E \in \mathcal S$;
> - if $E_1, E_2, \dots$ is a sequence of elements of $\mathcal S$, then $\bigcup_{k=1}^\infty E_k \in \mathcal S$.
>
> `[Axl 2.23]`

### Generated σ-algebra

> [!definition] Generated Sigma Algebra
> For a collection $\mathcal C$ of subsets of $X$, $\sigma(\mathcal C)$ is the intersection of all σ-algebras containing $\mathcal C$, the **σ-algebra generated by** $\mathcal C$ `[Bas pp. 12–13]` — a σ-algebra by the lemma below, and the smallest one containing $\mathcal C$.

### Borel Sigma Algebra

> [!definition] Borel Sigma Algebra
> If $X$ is a [[Topological Space]] and $\mathcal G$ is the collection of its open subsets, $\sigma(\mathcal G)$ is the **Borel σ-algebra** on $X$ `[Bas §2.1]`; its elements are the Borel sets. On $\mathbb R$: the smallest σ-algebra containing all the open sets `[Axl, Preface for Instructors]`.

## Property

> [!proposition] Contains $X$, closed under countable intersections
> $X = X \setminus \emptyset \in \mathcal S$, and $\bigcap_k E_k = X \setminus \bigcup_k (X \setminus E_k) \in \mathcal S$. `[Bas Def 2.1]` puts both in the definition.

> [!proposition] Intersections of σ-algebras — why "generated" makes sense
> If $\mathcal A_\alpha$ is a σ-algebra for each $\alpha$ in a non-empty index set $I$, then $\bigcap_{\alpha \in I} \mathcal A_\alpha$ is a σ-algebra. `[Bas Lemma 2.7]`
