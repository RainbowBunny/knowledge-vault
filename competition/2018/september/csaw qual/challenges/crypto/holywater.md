Let’s call the Lattice space $\mathcal{A}$:
This is how the crypto system works:

1. Generating $g, p$ of $\mathcal{A}$
2. Generates two random polynomials $V_A$ and $V_B$, along with random elements $e_A, f_A, e_B, f_B$ of $[1, 256]$.
3. Computes $$c = (V_A(g)^{e_A} \cdot p) \cdot V_A(g)^{f_A}$$and $$j = V_B(g)^{e_B} \cdot (p \cdot V_B(g)^{f_B})$$Then, $g, p, c, j$ is revealed.
4. Computes $$x = V_B(g)^{e_B} \cdot (((V_A(g)^{e_A} \cdot p) \cdot V_A(g)^{f_A}) \cdot V_B(g)^{f_B})$$ and uses this value to encrypt flag.

Observation 1: The operation is associate in this situation where the sub-algebra $\mathcal B$ generated by $g$ and $p$, then this sub-algebra has dimension 1, 2 or 4 (we can find the dimension is 4).

Observation 2: Similarly, the sub-algebra generated by $g$ is associate and commutative then this sub-algebra has dimension 1 or 2 (as $g$ is random then this is dimension 2). Thus, polynomial $V_B(g)^{e_B} = l_0 + l_1 g$ and $V_B(g)^{f_B} = r_0 + r_1 g$ where $l_0, r_0, l_1, r_1 \in \mathbb F_p$ 

Let $J(u) = V_B(g)^{e_B} \cdot u \cdot V_B(g)^{f_B}$  then $j = J(p), x = J(c)$. 
And we have:
$$J(u) = l_0 r_0 u + l_1 r_0 g u + l_0 r_1 u g + l_1 r_1 g u g$$
Having $j = J(p)$, we have:
$$j = J(p) = l_0 r_0 p + l_1 r_0 g p + l_0 r_1 p g + l_1 r_1 g p g$$
Because $\{p, gp, pg, gpg\}$ is a basis of the space $\mathcal B$, we can solve for $l_0 r_0, l_1 r_0, l_0 r_1, l_1 r_1$. Having these value, we can calculate:
$$J(1) = l_0 r_0 + l_1 r_0 g + l_0 r_1 g + l_1 r_1 g^2$$
$$J(g) = l_0 r_0 g + l_1 r_0 g^2 + l_0 r_1 g^2 + l_1 r_1 g^3$$
$$J(gp) = g J(p) = gj$$
Then we have a basis $\{1, g, p, gp\}$ to generate $c$ and thus we can calculate $J(c)$.

    mat = matrix(GF(P), basis)
    assert mat.rank() == 4
    b = matrix(GF(P), [v])

def solve():
